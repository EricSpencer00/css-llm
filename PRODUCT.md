# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People who encounter the “intern assignment” reel and want a quick, tactile web toy that turns its absurd AI claims into something they can poke at. They arrive curious and amused, usually on a laptop or phone, and should understand the joke within a few seconds.

## Product Purpose

This is a single-screen interactive demo for a genuinely CSS-executed small language model. Users seed a character-level recurrent network, watch the browser style engine resolve its fixed recurrent graph, and inspect the generated stylesheet. Success means the page makes the unusual implementation legible without pretending that JavaScript or a backend is doing the neural work.

## Brand Personality

Deadpan, technical, mischievous. The voice should sound like an intern confidently presenting a breakthrough that absolutely should not have passed review.

## Anti-references

Avoid the usual polished AI landing page: no gradient hero, floating glass cards, generic chatbot chrome, stock neural-network imagery, or vague enterprise claims. It should feel like a strange lab instrument with a sense of humor.

## Design Principles

- Make the joke legible before making the interface clever.
- Treat each impossible claim as a live system readout.
- Use real interaction to reward curiosity: typing and toggling should change the instrument.
- Keep the surface dense enough to feel engineered, but give the main joke room to breathe.
- Let the interface admit that it is a tiny, fixed-shape model.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet practical WCAG AA contrast targets, keep controls keyboard reachable with visible focus states, use labels that make sense without color, and respect prefers-reduced-motion. Avoid relying on tiny text for the core joke.
